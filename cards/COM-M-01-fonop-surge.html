COMPETITION Military (M)
COM-M-01
Freedom of Navigation Operation Surge
Escalation Risk
LOW
🟢
Actors: USAustralia
Action
The US and Australia increase FONOP tempo through South China Sea disputed waters — from quarterly to monthly operations, with Australian warships leading at least 50% of transits to demonstrate non-US allied commitment to rules-based maritime order.
Immediate Effect
China's Normalization Creep in the SCS is halted — occupied features cannot gain Legal status through unchallenged control. Australia gains +1 Indo-Pacific Presence bonus. China's ability to declare new Restricted Zones is blocked.
Potential Outcomes
Best Case
FONOP operations maintain the international legal precedent that China's SCS claims are not recognized; the UN Convention framework remains intact.
Partial Success
China harasses FONOP vessels with maritime militia and coast guard ships but does not engage kinetically; both sides demonstrate resolve without crossing into conflict.
Backfire
A Chinese coast guard vessel rams an Australian destroyer during a FONOP; Australia must decide whether to treat it as a casus belli or accept the incident diplomatically.
Counter-Cards
China Maritime Militia Surge (M)China Air Defense Identification Zone Declaration (D)
Constraints & Limitations
  • FONOP frequency has demonstrated diminishing returns — China treats each FONOP as evidence of provocation rather than deterrence, domestically mobilizing nationalist support
  • US destroyer availability limits the surge capacity — each forward-deployed DDG/CG is diverted from other missions
  • ASEAN nations privately ask the US to reduce FONOP frequency to avoid forcing them to take sides publicly
  • China has not changed its behavior in response to FONOPs — the legal challenge has not achieved behavioral change in 8+ years of operations
Key Assumptions
  • China doesn't escalate to firing on US vessels during the surge — a calculation that has held but requires continuous political management
  • FONOP legal precedents are being built for future use rather than achieving immediate behavioral change
  • Allied navies (UK, France, Australia, Japan) participate in the surge, multilateralizing the legal challenge
Strategic Notes
▼
US FONOPs in the SCS have been conducted since 2015. By 2030, Australian participation has evolved to Australian-led transits — a significant evolution of Australian military posture. FONOPs serve a specific legal purpose under UNCLOS — by physically transiting areas China claims as internal waters, allied navies preserve the international legal position that these are international waters. Without FONOPs, China's position could gain legal weight through acquiescence.
Historical Analogues
▼
  • **US Freedom of Navigation Program (1979-ongoing):** Reagan established systematic FONOPs against excessive maritime claims globally — created the institutional framework but with mixed behavioral change results
  • **1988 USS Yorktown Bumping Incident (Black Sea):** Soviet warship physically bumped a US destroyer conducting a FONOP in Soviet claimed waters — establishing that FONOPs can trigger kinetic responses short of armed conflict
  • **HMS Defender Black Sea FONOP (June 2021):** UK destroyer challenged Russia's claimed territorial waters off Crimea — Russia fired warning shots and bombed the ship's path, demonstrating escalation risk
