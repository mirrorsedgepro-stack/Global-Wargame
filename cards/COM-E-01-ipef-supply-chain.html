COM-E-01
IPEF Supply Chain Resilience Pact
Escalation Risk
LOW
🟢
Actors: USJapanAustraliaIndiaSingapore
Action
The US, Japan, Australia, India, and Singapore sign binding supply chain commitments within the Indo-Pacific Economic Framework (IPEF), creating preferential access rules and emergency sharing obligations for critical goods (semiconductors, critical minerals, pharmaceuticals, rare earths) — explicitly excluding China from the resilience network.
Immediate Effect
All five signatories become immune to supply chain disruption cards for 3 turns. China's ability to exploit supply chain dependencies is reduced by -2. The Pact creates an institutional framework for economic security cooperation requiring no further negotiation.
Potential Outcomes
Best Case
A fully functional alternative supply chain network operates independently of China; Western defense industries maintain production through any Chinese embargo.
Partial Success
The pact exists on paper but commercial interests prevent full implementation; companies continue to prefer cheaper Chinese sources when crises are not active.
Backfire
China accelerates its own Regional Comprehensive Supply Chain Agreement with ASEAN-7, offering better terms and pulling Malaysia, Vietnam, and Indonesia away from IPEF alignment.
Counter-Cards
China RCEP+ Supply Chain Initiative (E)
Constraints & Limitations
  • IPEF lacks market access commitments (no tariff concessions) — limiting attractiveness for developing nations that need export market access
  • India's industrial policy framework diverges from US free trade preferences on key sectors (agriculture, digital trade)
  • Implementation requires decades of investment to build alternative supply chains — the framework is a long-term project, not an immediate solution
  • China will specifically target IPEF members with preferential trade offers to erode the coalition
Key Assumptions
  • Participating nations prioritize security-informed supply chain decisions over pure economic efficiency
  • India provides a credible alternative manufacturing base — its 1.4B population and PLI schemes can scale to absorb relocated production
  • China doesn't offer a more attractive alternative framework (RCEP+, bilateral FTAs) that pulls ASEAN members away
Strategic Notes
▼
IPEF launched in 2022 but struggled with implementation — particularly the US refusal to offer market access commitments. By 2030, the Supply Chain Pillar has been strengthened through crisis-driven urgency. India providing an alternative manufacturing base is the most significant aspect — India's 1.4B population, growing middle class, and manufacturing ambitions make it the only country that can genuinely scale to replace Chinese manufacturing.
Historical Analogues
▼
  • **IPEF Launch (May 2022):** Biden launched IPEF with 13 founding members — but without market access commitments, trade ministers from Southeast Asia described it as 'NAFTA without the free trade part'
  • **Trans-Pacific Partnership (2016):** Obama's TPP was the more ambitious predecessor — its failure under Trump demonstrated that US trade framework commitment is a political variable, not a constant
  • **Marshall Plan (1948-1952):** The original economic framework alliance — $13B to rebuild Western Europe specifically to prevent Soviet economic capture — the model for using economic frameworks as strategic competition tools
