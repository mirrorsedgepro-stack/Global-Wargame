COMPETITION Diplomatic (D)
COM-D-01
Comprehensive Strategic Partnership Upgrade
Escalation Risk
LOW
🟢
Actors: ChinaASEAN Member
Action
China elevates its bilateral relationship with a key ASEAN member (likely Cambodia, Laos, or Myanmar) to Comprehensive Strategic Partnership status — the highest tier of Chinese diplomatic designation. The upgrade includes joint military exercises, intelligence sharing agreements, and preferential BRI investment packages.
Immediate Effect
Target ASEAN member switches from Hedging to China-Aligned status for 2 turns. ASEAN consensus on China-related resolutions becomes impossible. US loses -1 Regional Influence in Southeast Asia.
Potential Outcomes
Best Case
China secures a permanent veto within ASEAN decision-making, effectively preventing any ASEAN-level response to Chinese grey-zone operations in the SCS.
Partial Success
The target accepts economic benefits but resists military cooperation components; China gets trade alignment but not security alignment.
Backfire
The partnership announcement triggers domestic protests in the target country over sovereignty concerns; a nationalist government wins subsequent elections and reverses the upgrade.
Counter-Cards
US ASEAN Engagement Initiative (D)Japan Official Security Assistance (E)
Constraints & Limitations
  • ASEAN's consensus-based decision-making means Vietnam, Philippines, and Indonesia can block strong partnership language
  • The 'upgrade' means different things to different ASEAN members — Cambodia views it as alignment, Vietnam as economic opportunity, Philippines as security risk
  • US and Japan will apply diplomatic pressure on ASEAN members to reject or dilute the partnership terms
  • China's reputational damage from 2022 Solomon Islands controversy makes ASEAN members more cautious about high-profile partnership upgrades
Key Assumptions
  • ASEAN members' economic dependence on China (China is ASEAN's largest trading partner) outweighs their security concerns
  • The partnership upgrade is sufficiently vague that members can interpret it differently for domestic audiences
  • Vietnam and Philippines don't publicly oppose the upgrade in a way that prevents ASEAN consensus
Strategic Notes
▼
China's Comprehensive Strategic Partnership designation is the top tier of its five-level diplomatic classification system. By 2030, China has elevated 20+ nations to this status. The strategic value is ASEAN's consensus-based decision-making — a single aligned member can block regional responses. Cambodia's 2016 veto of SCS language at the ASEAN Summit demonstrated exactly this strategic value.
Historical Analogues
▼
  • **2003 China-ASEAN Strategic Partnership:** China became the first non-ASEAN nation to accede to ASEAN's Treaty of Amity and Cooperation — the foundational soft power move that this card upgrades
  • **2021 China-Iran 25-Year Comprehensive Cooperation Agreement:** China's template for deep partnership agreements that create economic and security dependency without formal treaty obligations
  • **ASEAN-China Free Trade Agreement (2010):** The zero-tariff agreement that made China ASEAN's largest trading partner and the economic foundation for diplomatic influence
