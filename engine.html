Game Rules & Reference

Overview

Global Wargame is a strategic card game set in 2030–2036 across the Indo-Pacific, the Middle East, Africa, Europe, and South Asia. Players represent nation-states and play action cards drawn from their country's real strategic options across the DIME domains. An adjudication engine resolves each card against the current world state and any defender played in response.

DIME Framework

D
Diplomatic — alliances, treaties, summits, UN maneuvering.
I
Informational — cyber, influence ops, intelligence, comms infrastructure.
M
Military — force posture, deployments, strikes, deterrence.
E
Economic — sanctions, trade, currency, supply chains, stockpiles.

Card Anatomy

  • Phase · Competition → Conflict → Crisis (stakes rise)
  • Domain · Primary DIME lane the card operates in
  • Risk · LOW · MEDIUM · HIGH · CRITICAL (cost & impact scale)
  • Actors · Nations required to play the card
  • Counter-cards · Cards that canonically answer this one

Adjudication Formula

1. Attacker base = RISK_MAG × PHASE_MULT
LOW=10 · MED=20 · HIGH=35 · CRI=55
COM×1.0 · CON×1.3 · CRI×1.6
2. Phase bonus — Conflict +4, Crisis +8
3. Actor posture — (DIME avg − 50) × 0.3
4. Defender value = base × 0.7, × 1.6 if canonical counter, × 1.15 if same domain
5. Defender posture — (DIME avg − 50) × 0.2 (subtracted)
6. Variance — deterministic hash of ids+turn, ±5

BACK

World State & Favourability

Each country tracks DIME posture (0–100 each), GDP, morale, and stockpiles of rare earths, semiconductors, energy, and grain.

               + 0.10 × Morale

Global Market

Four commodities track in real time: Rare Earths, Semiconductors, Energy, Grain. Every card moves the market — there is no “dud” play. Movement is a sum of two layers:

  • Layer A — Keyword: cards that explicitly mention a commodity (rare earth, chip, oil, grain, ASML, OPEC, fabrication, harvest…) get a strong, targeted hit on that commodity.
  • Layer B — Baseline ripple: every card always nudges all four commodities, weighted by its DIME domain — Economic plays touch everything, Military plays lean on energy/logistics, Information plays lean on semiconductors, Diplomatic plays ripple mildly across the board.

The Global Risk Index rises with Conflict and Crisis plays — high risk amplifies BACKFIRE consequences. End-of-turn: net exporters of a commodity gain GDP when its price rises; net importers feel the squeeze.

Economic Pressure Model

Each card is classified RESTRICTIVE (markets price in risk → prices rise, target stockpiles fall) or EXPANSIVE (markets price in supply → prices ease, actor stockpiles rise). The classifier uses four signals in priority:

  1. Meta-tag override — any M domain card → restrictive (military activity is destabilizing regardless of wording). Offensive I ops (cyber, hack, disinfo, espionage, breach, theft, prepositioning) → restrictive.
  2. Expansive vocabulary — partnership, accord, summit, mediation, sovereign fund, food security, integration, ceasefire, reset, pathway → expansive.
  3. Restrictive vocabulary — sanction, embargo, exclusion, exercise, drill, strike, militari[sz], ICBM, EMP, blockade, freeze, tariff, trigger, FONOP, mobilization, lockdown, violation → restrictive.
  4. Phase fallback — Competition → expansive · Conflict → restrictive · Crisis → restrictive.

BACKFIRE flips the sign of the move (a botched escalation gives back the priced-in risk premium).

Per-Card Effect Per Turn

Actor DIME — primary domain shifts ±15% × outcome mult.
Actor GDP — BEST +5%·base · PARTIAL +2%·base · BACKFIRE −10%·base.
Actor morale — ±4 pts × outcome mult.
Target GDP/morale — restrictive plays bruise target by ~4% GDP, −2 morale.
Global risk — COM ×0.15 · CON ×0.5 · CRI ×1.0. Diplomatic BEST plays without restrictive language reduce risk.
Markets & stockpiles — Layer A + Layer B (see “Global Market”).

Game Flow

  1. 2–4 players each pick a country
  2. Draw a 5-card hand filtered to your country
  3. Hover any card in your hand for a full-detail peek (id, summary, DIME profile, canonical counters)
  4. On your turn: play one card, or skip, or redraw
  5. Other players' hands are scanned — if any holds a canonical counter, a prompt lets them play it or pass
  6. Adjudicator resolves · world state updates · log records the action
  7. End of turn: market prices snapshot, stockpiles drift, risk decays toward baseline 30
  8. Net exporters of a commodity gain GDP when its price rises; net importers feel the squeeze
  9. 30 turns per player — long-horizon campaigns; effects compound
  10. Winner = highest favourability at end

Strategy Tips

  • Early Competition cards are safer — lock in posture before escalating.
  • CRITICAL cards have huge base scores but are easy targets for counters.
  • Favourability composites multiple axes — a weak GDP blunts otherwise strong DIME posture.
  • Stockpile-focused plays (rare earths, semis, energy) have knock-on market effects other cards benefit from.
  • A BACKFIRE is worse than skipping — know when to hold.
  • Over a 30-turn horizon, baseline ripple is cumulative — even subtle plays matter.
  • Net-exporter countries profit from rising prices on their stockpiles — escalate sectors where you're long, ease sectors where you're short.
  • Diplomatic BEST plays calm global risk — useful when your stockpiles are exposed.
